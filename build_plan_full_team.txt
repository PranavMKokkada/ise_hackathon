Prasoon: Biological Weather Engine (Service A)
Tech Stack: FastAPI backend with PostgreSQL (time-series) and Redis (cache). Use Prophet and LSTM
(TensorFlow/PyTorch) for forecasting, Scikit-learn for analytics. Integrate external APIs: OpenWeatherMap,
Twitter API, Google Trends, News API.
Service Architecture: Implement the following REST endpoints under /api/v1/biological-weather :
- Outbreak Predictions: GET /current (current risk scores), GET /forecast/{days} (7/14/21-day
forecasts), GET /region/{region_id} (region-specific forecasts), GET /timeline (time-series data).
- Environmental Drivers: GET /weather/{region_id} (temperature, humidity, rainfall), GET /
mobility/{region_id} (population movement), GET /breeding-zones (mosquito breeding risk
areas).
- Social Signals: GET /sentiment (Twitter/social sentiment on symptoms), GET /trends (Google
Trends spikes for symptoms), GET /news-triggers (recent outbreak news).
- Heatmap Data: GET /geojson (map rendering data), GET /hotspots (top 10 critical regions).
ML Tasks:
- Time-Series Forecasting: Build disease outbreak prediction models (7–21 days) using Prophet and LSTM.
- NLP Sentiment Analysis: Use BERT to analyze social media posts about relevant symptoms or concerns.
- Clustering: Apply K-means to identify geographic outbreak clusters for mapping hotspots.
- Correlation Analysis: Compute correlations between weather patterns (from environmental data) and
disease risk scores to feed into risk models.
Database Schema: Set up tables in PostgreSQL:
- outbreak_predictions: (id, region_id, disease_type, risk_score, predicted_cases, confidence_level,
prediction_date, forecast_days, created_at).
- environmental_data: (id, region_id, temperature, humidity, rainfall, air_quality_index, date, created_at).
- social_signals: (id, region_id, platform, keyword, sentiment_score, mention_count, trending, date).
Notes for Frontend Integration: Frontend screens will call Service A’s endpoints for outbreak and
environmental data. For example, on the Global Dashboard ( /dashboard ), use GET /biologicalweather/outbreak-predictions/current and GET /weather/{region} to populate the risk and
weather map layers. The Region Drill-Down ( /region/{id} ) will call GET /forecast/10 and GET /
environmental-drivers/weather/{id} to show local predictions and weather charts. The mobile
Public Health Cast ( /health-cast ) uses risk score and weather info from Service A ( /sentiment or /
trends ) for local alerts. Ensure CORS enabled so frontend can fetch these data.

Tech Stack: FastAPI backend with Neo4j (graph DB for supply chain network) and PostgreSQL (metadata).
Use Redis for caching. For data collection, employ Scrapy, BeautifulSoup, Selenium. Integrate APIs: global
news, trade data, company reports.
Service Architecture: Build /api/v1/supply-chain endpoints:
- Graph Data: GET /full-graph (complete supply chain graph), GET /disease/{disease_id}
(supply chain nodes for a disease), GET /node/{node_id} (details for a single node), GET /path/
{start}/{end} (find path between two nodes), GET /fragility-score (overall supply chain fragility).
- Disruptions: GET /current (active disruptions), GET /predicted (future disruptions), GET /byregion/{region_id} , POST /report (manual report), GET /history .
- Inventory: GET /global-status (global inventory levels), GET /region/{region_id} , GET /
item/{item_id} , POST /update (update inventory), GET /shortage-forecast .
- Suppliers: GET /all (list suppliers), GET /alternatives/{node_id} , GET /reliability-score ,
GET /capacity .
- Routing: POST /optimize (optimize supply routing), GET /recommendations (redistribution
suggestions), GET /logistics/{item_id} .
Graph Schema (Neo4j): Model nodes for Disease, Treatment, ActiveIngredient, Factory, RawMaterial,
Region, Supplier. Example relationships: (:Disease)-[:TREATED_BY]->(:Treatment) ,
(:Treatment)-[:CONTAINS]->(:ActiveIngredient) , etc. Store properties on relationships like
quantity, lead_time, reliability.
Database Schema (PostgreSQL):
- disruption_events: (id, node_id, node_type, disruption_type, severity, start_date, end_date, description,
source_url, verified, created_at).
- inventory_snapshots: (id, region_id, item_id, quantity, unit, timestamp, source, created_at).
- suppliers: (id, name, country, contact_info, reliability_score, capacity, specialization, certifications,
updated_at).
- scraping_log: (id, url, source, keywords_found, relevance_score, processed, created_at).
Data Collection Pipeline: Schedule automated scraping every 6 hours from sources like Reuters,
Bloomberg, government sites, and port statuses. Monitor keywords (“factory closure”, “strike”, “trade ban”,
etc). Use NLP to extract company names, locations, affected products, classify disruption type/severity, and
link events to Neo4j nodes.

ML Tasks:
1. Collision Detection:
- Consume outbreak forecasts from Service A and supply data from Service B every 30 minutes. Calculate
predicted demand vs available supply for each region/disease. If demand - supply > threshold ,
trigger alert. Output risk scores (0–100), severity levels, and affected items/regions.
Recommendation Engine (Reinforcement Learning):
Train a policy-gradient model where state includes current inventory, forecasts, supply status;
actions include redistributing stock, switching suppliers, stockpiling. Rewards minimize shortages
and maximize preparedness. Continuously update with historical outcome data.
Pattern Detection (Clustering & Association Rules):
Use clustering to find correlated events (e.g. “Flood in region X → shortage in Y”). Apply Apriori on
historical events and outcomes. Output patterns (e.g. “Holiday travel → flu spike”). Provide early
warning rules (to be fed into alerts).
Impact Simulation (Monte Carlo):
For what-if scenarios, run 1000 random simulations varying parameters (rainfall, mobility, etc.) to
generate probability distributions of outcomes. Provide confidence intervals for predicted cases,
supply shortages, etc.
Optimization (Linear Programming):
Solve redistribution optimization: objective minimize shortages and cost. Constraints: supplier
capacities, transportation lead times, demand requirements. Output optimal routing plans (fed to /
redistribution ).
